GAME DESIGN / SYSTEMS DESIGN / TABLETOP RPG

Monster Hunter — Tabletop RPG

The hunting experience, reimagined for the tabletop.

A personal tabletop RPG inspired by Monster Hunter. My goal was to capture preparation, tracking, learning monster behavior, and hunter progression through the decisions and storytelling of a tabletop RPG.

02 / THE HUNTING EXPERIENCE

A hunt begins well before combat.

I wanted to keep the main stages of Monster Hunter and give them a place in a story-driven campaign where decisions made on one expedition can shape the next.

  1. 01VillageGet ready to leave
  2. 02BriefingUnderstand the assignment
  3. 03PreparationChoose equipment
  4. 04ExplorationTrack the target
  5. 05CombatObserve and adapt
  6. 06ReturnPut discoveries to use

The complete loop is the design goal. The first playtest covered preparation, exploration, and combat.

03 / EXPLORATION & TRACKING

Track a creature through clues in the world.

My first prototype drew on older Monster Hunter games, with a region made up of 15 connected areas that both hunters and their target move through. Players could search for clues and decide which trail to follow.

01

Traverse

Move between areas while accounting for where the monster might go.

02

Observe

Look for tracks, compare clues, and read the environment.

03

Deduce

Decide where to track the target based on the information gathered.

LESSON FROM THE FIRST PLAYTEST

I found that copying the structure of the older games only took me so far. In a tabletop RPG, exploration needs to draw more on descriptions, interaction with the world, and player decisions. Exploration grounded more directly in the fiction is now a direction for redesign and is still in development.

04 / COMBAT & MONSTER DESIGN

The monster telegraphs danger. The hunters decide how to respond.

I wanted to translate animation reading and video game reflexes into a system for reading intent. The GM resolves the attack announced in the previous turn, then describes the tells for the attacks in the next turn. Once announced, an attack is committed.

01 / GM

Resolution

The monster performs its previously announced attack.

02 / GM

Telegraph

A cry, stance, or movement signals its next attack.

03 / PLAYERS

Anticipation

Hunters decide how to spend their four AP before the attack resolves.

CASE STUDY / LALA BARINA

Learning the monster is part of defeating it.

Lala Barina illustrates the interplay between telegraphs, enrage, paralysis, and breakable parts.

Each attack has a tell. Hunters learn to recognize these cues so they can reposition, dodge, or continue a combo.

Breaking certain parts changes its abilities. Its flower plays a role in petal attacks, and its stinger is involved in paralysis attacks. Choosing which part to target becomes a tactical decision.

WEAPON DESIGN

Weapons with their own rhythm.

Four weapons are planned, each with its own combos and constraints: Long Sword, Bow, Insect Glaive, and Dual Blades. Managing AP, combos, and positioning should preserve each weapon’s identity in a turn-based system.

05 / EQUIPMENT & PROGRESSION

Each hunt prepares the next.

Progression is based on crafting and combining gear. Weapons, armor, and skills are intended to let hunters customize their playstyle and put together gear combinations suited to their next targets.

EQUIPMENT

Develop your build

The design includes several craftable pieces per monster and set bonuses that encourage combining pieces.

CAMPAIGN

Progress beyond the character

Resources, hunts, and player choices are also intended to contribute to the village’s development.

The design bible describes these systems. The first playtest covered only part of the full loop.

06 / WORLD & CAMPAIGN

A world beyond the hunt.

The campaign takes place in a world of regions, kingdoms, and territories. The village anchors progression. Expeditions are meant to affect the inhabitants, resources, and opportunities available to the players.

Giving expeditions a purpose.

The world connects individual hunts to a larger story. Monsters have territories, behaviors, and roles in their ecosystems.

Over the campaign, hunters’ discoveries and choices are intended to shape the village and the wider world.

This narrative framework opens up stakes beyond defeating a creature.

07 / PLAYTEST & ITERATION

Testing the design in play.

A first playtest with three players put preparation, exploration, and combat to the test. My assessment as designer and GM is provisional. The overall structure seemed to work, while several aspects need iteration.

DESIGN INTENT RETAINED

Long encounters

The length of the encounters matches the intended experience. The challenge is to keep decisions engaging throughout.

TO INVESTIGATE

Repetition across turns

Action cycles deliberately resemble those of Monster Hunter. I still need to determine whether repeating them stays engaging at the table.

REDESIGN DIRECTION

Exploration grounded more directly in the fiction

Moving between areas and searching for clues need more interaction and storytelling to work at the table.

NEEDS REVISION

Balance

I think the values and interactions need a substantial revision before the next tests.

PROVISIONAL FINDINGS

These findings reflect my own observations as designer. Detailed player feedback is still pending. This overview distinguishes documented systems, what was tested, and areas for improvement.
