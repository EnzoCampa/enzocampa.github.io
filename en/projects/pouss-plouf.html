02 / CONTEXT

A game designed for a real building façade.

Pouss’Plouf ! was designed for Cœur de Ville en Lumières in Montpellier. The façade, over 30 metres wide, directly influenced the level design. Its nine windows became holes in the ice floe, and the central doorway informed obstacle placement and the match display.

9 windows

Openings built into the arena and activated at random

5 holes at once

The maximum reached as the match progresses

3 minutes

Short matches designed for a public event

03 / GAMEPLAY

Slide, push, score.

Two teams of two face off on an ice floe. Each team shares one controller to control two animals. The goal is to push opponents into openings in the arena and finish the three-minute match with the highest score.

Physics-based movement

Sliding, momentum, acceleration, and steering

Player collisions

Push opponents into holes to score

Dynamic arena

Holes open and close throughout the match

04 / MY CONTRIBUTION

Building movement and character systems.

I developed the movement system from its early velocity-based versions to a physics-based approach using Add Force. I also worked on rotation, respawning, temporary immunity, early collision prototypes, and several interface features.

05 / FINDINGS AND LESSONS

Playtesters liked the movement. Collision fairness still needed work.

Around six to seven playtest sessions informed the iterations. Testers were satisfied with the final movement feel. Collisions felt better, but fairness remained uneven. A slower character could sometimes push a faster opponent.

Result

Movement feel

Adjustments to sliding, speed, and braking produced controls that testers liked.

Known limitation

Impact consistency

Another developer corrected and completed the final collision system. The impacts felt satisfying, although some situations still felt unfair.
